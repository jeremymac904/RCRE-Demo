# AI, Automation, and CRM

## Current Adoption

NAR's 2025 Technology Survey found that AI use among Realtors had become common.

Twenty percent reported daily AI use.

Twenty two percent reported weekly use.

Twenty seven percent reported use a few times per month.

Thirty two percent reported not actively using AI.

Seventeen percent reported a significantly positive business impact and thirty three percent reported a moderately positive impact.

The lesson for coaching is clear.

Access is not the advantage.

Implementation is the advantage.

## CRM Rule

Every meaningful prospect should have:

Source.

Status.

Last contact.

Next action.

Timeline.

Notes.

Consent information.

The CRM should tell the agent who to contact and why.

## Automation Rule

Automate repetition, not relationships.

Good automation:

Appointment confirmation.

Reminder.

Lead routing.

Task creation.

Review request trigger.

Email sequence.

Database birthday reminder.

Post closing nurture.

Bad automation:

Fake personal messages.

Unreviewed market advice.

Unverified legal statements.

Mass spam.

Fabricated personalization.

## Human Review

The agent must review AI generated:

Market statistics.

Property facts.

Legal explanations.

Compensation language.

Financing language.

Advertising claims.

Testimonials.

Neighborhood descriptions.

Any content that could create fair housing risk.

## Privacy

Do not put sensitive client information into an AI tool without understanding the tool's data handling and brokerage policy.

Avoid uploading:

Social Security numbers.

Bank statements.

Tax returns.

Passwords.

Unredacted loan files.

Medical information.

Other sensitive documents.

## AI Coaching Use

A custom coaching Project can help:

Prepare weekly review.

Analyze scorecard.

Practice scripts.

Draft action plan.

Create content plan.

Review conversion.

Track commitments supplied by the user.

The AI coach should complement human judgment, not claim professional authority it does not have.
