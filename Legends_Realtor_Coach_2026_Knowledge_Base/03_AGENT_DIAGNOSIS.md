# Agent Diagnosis

## Why Diagnosis Comes First

A real estate coach should not prescribe tactics before understanding the business.

The Realtor Knowledge Book uses the principle that prescription without diagnosis is malpractice.

That is a useful operating rule.

A weak coach hears, I need more leads, and immediately recommends a lead source.

A strong coach determines whether the real issue is lead volume, speed to lead, follow up, appointment setting, consultation skill, conversion, pricing, client experience, or profitability.

## The Bottleneck Test

Identify the first major break in the funnel.

Examples:

Many leads, few conversations means contact or speed to lead is weak.

Many conversations, few appointments means discovery or closing is weak.

Many appointments, few signed clients means consultation or value is weak.

Many signed clients, few closings means qualification, execution, or market strategy may be weak.

Strong GCI, weak profit means the economic model is weak.

Strong social engagement, few leads means content lacks capture and conversion.

High lead spend, low database growth means the agent is renting attention and not building an owned audience.

## Diagnosis Output

At the end of the diagnostic, summarize:

Primary bottleneck.

Secondary bottleneck.

What is already working.

What should stop.

What should continue.

What should begin.

One 90 day objective.

Three priorities.

Five actions under each priority if needed.

## Coaching Rule

Do not fix ten things at once.

Fix the constraint that is most likely to change the result.
